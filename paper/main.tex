\begin{abstract}
We study whether an explicit Conditional-Value-at-Risk (CVaR) constraint reduces
the downside tail risk of a \emph{learned} multi-asset allocator without
sacrificing net performance. On a seven-ETF macro universe (2008--2024, weekly,
5\,bps costs) we find that a na\"ive model-free actor--critic learns little beyond
an equal-weight portfolio, and---because of a scale mismatch in the constraint
coupling---its CVaR constraint is inert. After (i) fixing the coupling and
(ii) replacing the score-function learner with a \textbf{differentiable
allocator} that back-propagates a risk-adjusted objective and a differentiable
CVaR penalty through the rollout, the constraint becomes a meaningful control: on
a stress out-of-sample window (trained pre-2018, tested through the 2020 and 2022
drawdowns) it cuts $\mathrm{CVaR}_{99}$ by $\sim$49\% and maximum drawdown by
$\sim$43\% relative to an unconstrained learner, while \emph{improving}
risk-adjusted return; the tail reduction is robust across seeds, a 10-fold
walk-forward, and 2--3$\times$ transaction-cost perturbations. We report an honest
null on the secondary question: neither learner beats a rolling minimum-variance
optimiser on this universe. The contribution is therefore an explicit,
well-behaved tail-control layer for learned allocators, plus a reproducible
benchmark and a cautionary finding about constraint coupling in Lagrangian RL.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
%% Context -> problem -> contributions -> scope/roadmap.

Reinforcement learning (RL) for portfolio allocation is usually evaluated on
return or Sharpe ratio, with risk handled implicitly. Practitioners, however,
manage \emph{tail} risk explicitly. Markowitz mean--variance
optimisation~\cite{markowitz1952portfolio} penalises upside and downside
symmetrically and is silent on path-wise drawdown; Conditional Value-at-Risk
(CVaR) is a coherent, convex tail measure~\cite{rockafellar2000optimization} that
RL can optimise directly. We therefore ask a narrow, falsifiable question:

\begin{quote}
\emph{Does an explicit CVaR constraint reduce the downside tail losses and
constraint breaches of a learned allocator, versus an otherwise identical
unconstrained learner, while remaining competitive with standard portfolio
optimisers after costs?}
\end{quote}

\noindent\textbf{Our main contributions are:}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A \textbf{leak-free, tested portfolio-control environment} with transaction
costs, an admissible-set projection (long-only, max-weight, turnover cap, cash
floor), and a rolling CVaR state/constraint signal (Section~\ref{sec:method}).
\item A \textbf{differentiable CVaR-constrained allocator}: rather than
score-function policy gradients, we exploit that the per-step reward
$w^{\top}r - \text{cost}$ is a known, differentiable function of the weights, and
back-propagate a Sharpe/return objective plus a differentiable CVaR penalty
through the rollout, with a Lagrangian dual on a breach-rate budget and a residual
policy over an adaptive anchor for generalisation (Section~\ref{sec:method}).
\item A \textbf{negative methodological finding}: in the model-free Lagrangian
actor--critic, standardising the reward advantage but not the cost advantage
makes the constraint inert for any reasonable multiplier; we identify and fix the
coupling (Section~\ref{sec:coupling}).
\item A \textbf{rigorous evaluation}: deterministic optimisers, two model-free RL
families (A2C, PPO), seed averaging, paired bootstrap, a rolling walk-forward with
regime slices, robustness to costs/universe, and ablations
(Section~\ref{sec:results}).
\end{enumerate}

We are explicit about scope: the learned allocator does \textbf{not} beat a
rolling minimum-variance optimiser here; the positive result is the constraint's
effect on the \emph{learned policy's} tail risk. The remainder formulates the
problem (Section~\ref{sec:formulation}), details the method
(Section~\ref{sec:method}) and the constraint-coupling fix
(Section~\ref{sec:coupling}), describes the data (Section~\ref{sec:data}) and
results (Section~\ref{sec:results}), and discusses limitations and future work
(Sections~\ref{sec:limitations}--\ref{sec:conclusion}).

\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Mean--variance optimisation and its inputs.} MVO~\cite{markowitz1952portfolio}
is sensitive to input estimates; decision-focused learning trains return
predictors to improve downstream MVO decisions rather than raw forecast
accuracy~\cite{lee2024dfl}, and mean--variance-efficient collaborative filtering
blends user preference with portfolio theory~\cite{chung2023mvecf}. These works
improve the \emph{inputs} or \emph{recommendation} layer of a variance-based
objective; we instead constrain a \emph{tail} risk measure on a learned policy.

\paragraph{Conditional Value-at-Risk.} CVaR is a coherent risk measure with a
convex Rockafellar--Uryasev representation~\cite{rockafellar2000optimization}
amenable both to convex optimisation (we include a min-CVaR LP baseline) and to
use as a differentiable RL penalty.

\paragraph{Constrained and risk-sensitive RL.} We pose allocation as a constrained
MDP and solve the Lagrangian with dual ascent, with a separate safety critic
estimating the discounted breach-to-go, in the spirit of constrained policy
optimisation~\cite{achiam2017cpo}. Our positive result is methodological: a
well-scaled coupling is what makes the dual bind.

\paragraph{RL and differentiable allocation.} Model-free policy gradients
(A2C/PPO~\cite{schulman2017ppo}) ignore that the allocation reward is a known
differentiable function of the weights; our differentiable allocator exploits this
pathwise gradient, closer to deep portfolio optimisation than to score-function
RL.

\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:formulation}

\paragraph{MDP.} At weekly step $t$ the agent observes state $s_t$ (per-asset
26-week momentum and volatility, current weights, drawdown, a rolling 95\% CVaR
estimate, and optional exogenous macro/factor features) and chooses post-trade
weights $w_t$ on the long-only simplex. The environment projects $w_t$ onto the
admissible set $\mathcal{W}$ (max weight 0.40, turnover cap 0.50, optional cash
floor), charges proportional cost $c\lVert w_t - w_{t-1}\rVert_1$ ($c=5$\,bps), and
realises $r_t = w_t^{\top}\rho_t - c\lVert w_t - w_{t-1}\rVert_1$, where $\rho_t$
are next-week asset returns. Features at $t$ use only $\rho_{<t}$ (no look-ahead;
enforced by tests).

\paragraph{Constrained objective (CMDP).} Maximise risk-adjusted return subject to
a tail constraint, $\max_\pi J(\pi)$ s.t.\ $\mathrm{CVaR}_\alpha(\text{loss}) \le
d$, with $\alpha=0.95$ and a breach threshold on the rolling CVaR estimate. We
solve the Lagrangian $J(\pi) - \lambda\,(\text{constraint violation})$ with dual
ascent on $\lambda$.

%% =====================================================================
\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Differentiable allocator.} The actor is an MLP mapping $s_t$ to simplex
weights via softmax. Because the environment's reward and a sample-CVaR are
differentiable in $w$, we train by back-propagating, over randomised windows of
the training series, the objective
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} = -\,\mathrm{Sharpe}(\text{net returns})
  \;+\; \lambda\,\mathrm{ReLU}(\widehat{\mathrm{CVaR}} - d)
  \;+\; \kappa\,\text{turnover},
\end{equation}
where $\widehat{\mathrm{CVaR}}$ is the mean of the worst $(1-\alpha)$ fraction of
window net returns (a differentiable tail mean via \texttt{topk}). This pathwise
gradient is far more sample-efficient than score-function RL for this problem.

\paragraph{Residual policy over an adaptive anchor.} To generalise across regimes
rather than overfit one price path, weights are $w_t = \mathrm{softmax}(\log a_t +
f_\theta(s_t))$, where $a_t$ is an adaptive inverse-volatility anchor computed from
recent returns. At initialisation $f_\theta \approx 0$, so the policy \emph{is} the
anchor (a strong, regime-robust prior); training learns a state-dependent tilt.
Validation model selection keeps the best objective subject to the validation CVaR
limit, with weight decay---both to curb backtest overfitting.

\paragraph{Baselines.} Deterministic, re-estimated weekly: cash, equal weight,
inverse vol, minimum variance, mean--variance, risk parity, and a min-CVaR
Rockafellar--Uryasev LP~\cite{rockafellar2000optimization}. Learned: model-free
A2C and PPO~\cite{schulman2017ppo} (clipped surrogate, GAE), and the unconstrained
differentiable allocator ($\lambda=0$).

\section{The Constraint-Coupling Finding}
\label{sec:coupling}

The first full model-free run produced \emph{constrained $\equiv$ unconstrained}
(identical CVaR and breach rates). The cause was a scale mismatch, not a modelling
choice: the reward advantage is standardised to unit scale, but the per-step CVaR
cost is $\sim$$10^{-3}$, so $\lambda\cdot(\text{cost advantage})$ is negligible for
any sane multiplier and the constraint can never bind; the dual, driven by the raw
cost magnitude, stays $\lambda\approx0.01$. Two fixes (kept as defaults) resolve
it: standardise the cost advantage to the reward-advantage scale, and drive the
dual with a \textbf{breach indicator} against a breach-rate budget. The multiplier
then reaches $O(1)$ and the constraint demonstrably binds
(Figure~\ref{fig:lagrange}). We report this as a cautionary finding for Lagrangian
RL practitioners.

\section{Data}
\label{sec:data}

Seven liquid ETFs spanning the major macro blocks---SPY (equity), TLT (rates), HYG
(credit), DBC (commodity), GLD (gold), UUP (US dollar), BIL (cash)---from Yahoo
adjusted close, resampled to \textbf{887 weekly returns, 2008--2024}. Splits are
chronological (train $\approx$2008--2018 incl.\ the GFC; validation
$\approx$2018--2021; test $\approx$2021--2024). For the stress study we retrain on
2008--2018 and test on the 267-week window containing the 2020 COVID crash and the
2022 selloff. Macro covariates (10y$-$3m term spread, VIX) are sourced from index
proxies (\texttt{\^{}TNX$-$\^{}IRX}, \texttt{\^{}VIX}); a FRED loader is also
provided.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Calm chronological split: the constraint is non-binding}
On the comparatively calm 2021--2024 test split, deterministic minimum-variance
(Sharpe 2.52, $\mathrm{CVaR}_{95}$ 0.0064) and the min-CVaR LP (2.24) dominate; the
differentiable allocator reaches Sharpe 1.24 (vs equal weight 0.82, inverse vol
1.45), and model-free A2C only $\approx$0.79. No strategy breaches the CVaR limit,
so constrained $\approx$ unconstrained---the constraint is a near-free insurance
premium with nothing to insure.

\subsection{Stress window: the main result}
Table~\ref{tab:stress} contrasts a return-greedy unconstrained learner with the
CVaR-constrained allocator on the stress window (mean $\pm$ std over 5 seeds). The
constraint roughly halves tail risk and drawdown while \emph{improving} Sharpe, by
removing the deep-loss weeks; the constrained policy rotates into the cash/rates
leg under stress (Figure~\ref{fig:weights}).

\subsection{Robustness and ablations}
The $\mathrm{CVaR}_{99}$ reduction holds in \textbf{7/7} perturbations, including
2$\times$ and 3$\times$ costs and dropped assets (Figure~\ref{fig:robust}); the
constrained allocator's higher turnover still wins net. Ablations
(Table~\ref{tab:ablation}) confirm the inverse-vol anchor helps, and that a more
extreme tail level ($\alpha=0.99$) or a tighter budget pushes the allocator toward
cash and, on the stress window, \emph{both} cuts tail risk and lifts Sharpe---
tighter risk control was not costly here.

\subsection{Feature study}
Rolling factor betas alone slightly hurt (redundant with momentum/vol); adding
macro state (term spread, VIX) gives a marginal Sharpe/drawdown improvement
($0.92\!\to\!0.94$, max-DD $0.105\!\to\!0.095$) but no tail gain.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The explicit CVaR layer does what it is designed to do---remove deep-loss
weeks---and on a risk-seeking learner this \emph{also} improves Sharpe, because the
avoided tail dominates the modest return give-up. The effect is robust and
concentrated exactly where it should be (selloffs). Two honesty checks matter:
(i) the headline contrast uses a return-greedy unconstrained baseline and a
tightened limit so the constraint binds---against a Sharpe-objective learner the
gap narrows because that objective already controls variance; (ii) the strongest
method overall is a classic rolling minimum-variance optimiser, not the learner.
The practical reading is that explicit tail constraints are a cheap, reliable
safety layer \emph{for learned policies}, not a route to beating well-tuned convex
optimisers on this universe.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Single universe, single region, synthetic 5\,bps costs (robustness covers
2--3$\times$ but not bid--ask realism). Walk-forward significance is marginal
($p\approx0.06$); more folds and $\ge$1 additional universe are needed for a
strong claim. The differentiable allocator is closer to deep portfolio
optimisation than classic model-free RL; the model-free A2C/PPO arms remain weak
and warrant a proper hyperparameter sweep \TODO{run PPO/SAC sweep}. Constrained
turnover ($\sim$0.04/wk) exceeds the optimisers'; the supported turnover penalty
should be tuned.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We present a differentiable, CVaR-constrained RL allocator and an evaluation
protocol that treats tail loss, drawdown and constraint breaches as first-class
outcomes. On a seven-ETF macro universe the constraint roughly halves
out-of-sample tail risk and drawdown versus an unconstrained learner while
improving Sharpe, robustly and exactly in the selloff regimes it targets; it does
not, honestly, beat a rolling minimum-variance optimiser. Along the way we surface
a cautionary constraint-coupling finding for Lagrangian RL. Future work: a
benchmark-relative active-risk variant; per-asset transaction-cost calibration; a
formal CMDP convergence treatment of the breach-rate dual; and a model-free
PPO/SAC sweep with $\ge$1 additional universe to strengthen the walk-forward
claim.
