\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Two failure modes affect any constrained financial RL study.} (i)~An
\textbf{evaluation-protocol reversal}: on a 31-asset universe, a single stress window
ranks the learned allocator above every optimiser while a 20-fold rolling walk-forward
inverts the ranking (Wilcoxon $p=6\times10^{-4}$)---apparent ``beat-the-optimiser'' claims
can be protocol artefacts. (ii)~A \textbf{Lagrangian constraint-coupling failure}: standardising
the reward advantage but not the cost advantage renders the constraint silently inert for
any reasonable multiplier, making the CVaR budget nominal rather than enforced.
We identify, name, and fix both using a differentiable CVaR-constrained portfolio allocator
on a seven-ETF macro universe (2008--2024, weekly, 5\,bps costs). With the coupling failure
repaired, the constraint acts as a genuine tail safety layer: through the 2020 crash
and 2022 selloff it cuts $\mathrm{CVaR}_{99}$ by $49\%$ and max drawdown by $43\%$
while improving Sharpe by $40\%$ and eliminating hard constraint violations. A 40-fold
walk-forward confirms constrained $\mathrm{CVaR}_{99}$ is lower in 26/40 folds. The constraint enables a regime-switching hybrid that \emph{does} beat rolling minimum-variance
($+0.107$ Sharpe, robust across the volatility-quantile axis)---the constraint mechanism
is what makes this possible, not a lucky threshold. The two named failure modes are
transferable to any CVaR-constrained financial RL system; identifying and fixing them
is the contribution.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
%% Context -> problem -> contributions -> scope/roadmap.

In the week of 16 March 2020, the S\&P 500 fell another 15\% in four days as
COVID-19 lockdowns shut economies worldwide.  A learned portfolio allocator trained
on the preceding decade, with no explicit rule about how much loss it was budgeted
to absorb, had no mechanical reason to rotate into Treasuries or cash when the VIX
tripled overnight.  Absent a tail limit, the policy kept holding equities through
the worst losses.  An otherwise-identical allocator carrying an explicit
Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR) limit had already rotated into the cash/rates leg
before the trough and emerged with roughly half the drawdown (11.8\% peak-to-trough
versus 20.6\% for its unconstrained twin, Table~\ref{tab:stress}).

That difference is a result, not the headline. An explicit CVaR constraint is
a safety layer that cuts tail losses of a \emph{learned} allocator without
sacrificing risk-adjusted return.  There is, however, a catch.  In the first
model-free implementation we tried, a standard Lagrangian
actor--critic, the \emph{dual channel was inert}: the Lagrange multiplier stayed
pinned near zero, so the constraint exerted no explicit tail pressure through the
mechanism that was supposed to enforce it. The culprit is a subtle scale mismatch
that we describe, name, and fix in Section~\ref{sec:coupling}. It is likely present
in any Lagrangian RL system that standardises the return advantage but not the cost
advantage. (As Section~\ref{sec:coupling} shows, the constrained \emph{architecture}
still regularises the tail through its safety-critic and projection structure even
when the multiplier is inert, the policies are \emph{not} numerically identical, but
the Lagrangian mechanism itself never engages.)

Reinforcement learning for portfolio allocation is usually evaluated on return
or Sharpe ratio, with risk handled implicitly.  Practitioners, however, manage
\emph{tail} risk explicitly.  Markowitz mean--variance
optimisation~\cite{markowitz1952portfolio} penalises upside and downside
symmetrically and is silent on path-wise drawdown. CVaR is a coherent, convex tail
measure~\cite{rockafellar2000optimization} that RL can optimise directly.  We ask
a narrow, falsifiable question.

\begin{quote}
\emph{Does an explicit CVaR constraint reduce the downside tail losses and
constraint breaches of a learned allocator, versus an otherwise identical
unconstrained learner, while remaining competitive with standard portfolio
optimisers after costs?}
\end{quote}

\noindent\textbf{We make the following contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A differentiable CVaR-constrained allocator.} On a leak-free, tested
portfolio-control environment (transaction costs, an admissible-set projection, and a
rolling CVaR state and constraint), we back-propagate a return objective plus a
differentiable CVaR penalty through the rollout, with a Lagrangian dual on a
breach-rate budget and a residual policy over an adaptive anchor (Section~\ref{sec:method}).
\item \textbf{A constraint that demonstrably binds, with a defined domain of superiority.}
The constraint cuts tail risk without sacrificing risk-adjusted return, and a
regime-switching hybrid (constrained RL in high-volatility weeks, min-variance otherwise)
reaches walk-forward Sharpe $1.316$ vs $1.209$ for pure min-variance, a $2.5\times$ Sharpe
advantage in the high-volatility regime where the tail constraint is most active
(Sections~\ref{sec:results},~\ref{sec:discussion}).
\item \textbf{A constraint-coupling failure mode.} In the model-free Lagrangian
actor--critic, standardising the reward advantage but not the cost advantage makes the
constraint inert for any reasonable multiplier. We name and fix it, a diagnostic that
transfers to any constrained financial RL (Section~\ref{sec:coupling}).
\item \textbf{An evaluation-protocol reversal.} On a 31-asset universe a single stress
window ranks the learned allocator above every optimiser, while a rolling walk-forward
inverts the ranking (min-variance Sharpe $1.40$ vs $0.74$), so apparent
``beat-the-optimiser'' results can be protocol artifacts (Section~\ref{sec:protocol}). Our
evaluation, deterministic optimisers plus three tuned model-free RL families under a
two-universe rolling walk-forward with Deflated-Sharpe and multiple-testing control, is
built to avoid exactly this trap.
\end{enumerate}

We are explicit about scope. The pure learned allocator does \textbf{not} beat a
rolling minimum-variance optimiser in aggregate. The hybrid \textbf{does} ($+0.107$
Sharpe), exploiting the regime in which the tail constraint is most active. But a
horse-race win is \emph{not} the contribution. The contribution is methodological
and transferable. A differentiable constraint mechanism that demonstrably binds and
cuts tails, and two failure modes, constraint-coupling and protocol-reversal, that
any constrained-RL-for-finance study must control for or risk reporting an artifact.
The allocator is the testbed that makes both failure modes visible and fixable.

\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Mean--variance optimisation and its inputs} MVO~\cite{markowitz1952portfolio}
is sensitive to input estimates. Decision-focused learning trains return
predictors to improve downstream MVO decisions rather than raw forecast
accuracy~\cite{lee2024dfl}, and mean--variance-efficient collaborative filtering
blends user preference with portfolio theory~\cite{chung2023mvecf}. These works
improve the \emph{inputs} or \emph{recommendation} layer of a variance-based
objective. We instead constrain a \emph{tail} risk measure on a learned policy.

\paragraph{Conditional Value-at-Risk} $\mathrm{CVaR}_\alpha$ is the \emph{expected
portfolio loss on the worst $(1{-}\alpha)$ fraction of outcomes}, the average of all
losses that exceed the $\alpha$-quantile, known as Value-at-Risk ($\mathrm{VaR}_\alpha$).
Unlike variance it ignores upside. Unlike $\mathrm{VaR}$ it averages across the entire
tail rather than reporting a single threshold.  Rockafellar and
Uryasev~\cite{rockafellar2000optimization} show it has the tractable form
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{CVaR}_\alpha(L) \;=\; \min_{\zeta\in\mathbb{R}}\!\left\{
    \zeta + \frac{1}{1-\alpha}\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\max(L-\zeta,\,0)\right]
  \right\},
\end{equation}
where $L$ denotes the portfolio loss and the inner expectation is over the empirical
loss distribution.  The minimum is achieved at $\zeta=\mathrm{VaR}_\alpha$, the
representation admits a convex LP (we include this as a baseline) and a differentiable
penalty via \texttt{topk}.

\paragraph{RL for portfolio management}
The dominant pattern in RL-for-portfolio literature is to train on a single historical
window, evaluate on one held-out test split, and compare against equal-weight or
buy-and-hold.
Jiang et al.~\cite{jiang2017deep} introduce the EIIE architecture for cryptocurrency
portfolio allocation, comparing against twelve classical strategies on a single
$\approx$50-day test window, no walk-forward, no statistical testing, no tail-risk
constraint, and no minimum-variance baseline.
Ye et al.~\cite{ye2020sarl} augment the state with sentiment signals (SARL) and report
higher accumulated return on a fixed evaluation period, again with no cross-validation
and no tail-risk metric.
Liu et al.~\cite{liu2021finrl} (FinRL) provide a reusable RL-for-trading framework
that includes a minimum-variance baseline and supports rolling retraining, but the
primary demonstrations use a single train/test split and report no significance tests
or seed-averaged results.
Most closely, Huang~\cite{huang2025ctrl} applies continuous-time RL to asset--liability
management and benchmarks against six classical and RL baselines, but targets a quadratic
surplus objective rather than an explicit tail constraint.
None of these works uses CVaR as a constraint, seeds multiple runs, or applies
statistical testing.
\emph{To our knowledge, this is the first work to subject RL portfolio allocation to a
40-fold, two-universe rolling walk-forward with Wilcoxon testing and Benjamini--Hochberg
multiple-testing control,} directly exposing the protocol-reversal artefact that
single-window evaluations conceal.

\paragraph{Direct reinforcement and pathwise gradients}
Moody and Saffell~\cite{moody2001learning} first trained a recurrent trading policy
by back-propagating the \emph{differential Sharpe ratio} directly through the return
function, an exact pathwise gradient predating deep learning.  Buehler et
al.~\cite{buehler2019deephedging} (Deep Hedging) extend this to neural derivative
hedging, back-propagating CVaR as a \emph{utility} (a risk-aversion coefficient)
through a simulated trajectory.  Our differentiable allocator inherits the
pathwise-gradient insight from both, but replaces the risk-aversion coefficient
with an explicit CVaR \emph{budget constraint}. The practitioner specifies a
tolerable loss threshold directly, and a dual variable enforces it.  Model-free
policy gradients (A2C/PPO~\cite{schulman2017ppo}) and off-policy actor--critics
(SAC~\cite{haarnoja2018soft}) ignore the differentiable structure of the portfolio
reward entirely. Our allocator is strictly more sample-efficient for this problem
class.  Recent work makes such policies auditable. ProtoHedge~\cite{faloughi2025protohedge}
replaces the black-box hedger with a prototype-similarity policy at negligible
performance cost.

\paragraph{CVaR-constrained RL}
Chow et al.~\cite{chow2018riskconstrained} derive the Lagrangian gradient for CVaR-constrained
CMDPs. Tessler et al.~\cite{tessler2019rcpo} (RCPO) and Ying et al.~\cite{ying2022cppo} (CPPO)
implement hard CVaR constraints for robotics control tasks.  None of these works identifies
the \emph{constraint-coupling failure} we describe. Standardising return-advantage to unit scale
without normalising the cost advantage makes the Lagrange multiplier permanently inert, a failure
specific to portfolio allocation where returns are $O(10^{-3})$ and breach indicators are $O(1)$,
invisible in robotics.  We pose allocation as a constrained MDP~\cite{achiam2017cpo} and show that
the well-scaled dual is what makes the constraint actually bind.
The closest concurrent work is \citet{dong2025clauselens} (\textsc{ClauseLens}), a
CVaR-constrained RL system for reinsurance pricing; our contribution is orthogonal---we
name the \textbf{Lagrangian constraint-coupling failure} and \textbf{evaluation-protocol
reversal} as transferable failure modes that affect any CVaR-constrained financial RL
system.

\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:formulation}

\paragraph{MDP} At weekly step $t$ the agent observes state $s_t$ (per-asset
26-week momentum and volatility, current weights, drawdown, a rolling 95\% CVaR
estimate, and optional exogenous macro/factor features) and chooses post-trade
weights $w_t$ on the long-only simplex. The environment projects $w_t$ onto the
admissible set $\mathcal{W}$ (max weight 0.40, turnover cap 0.50, optional cash
floor), charges proportional cost $c\lVert w_t - w_{t-1}\rVert_1$ ($c=5$\,bps), and
realises $r_t = w_t^{\top}\rho_t - c\lVert w_t - w_{t-1}\rVert_1$, where $\rho_t$
are next-week asset returns. Features at $t$ use only $\rho_{<t}$ (no look-ahead,
enforced by tests).

\paragraph{Constrained objective (CMDP)} A \emph{constrained Markov Decision
Process} (CMDP)~\cite{achiam2017cpo} augments the standard MDP with one or more
inequality constraints that the policy must satisfy on average.  We maximise
risk-adjusted return subject to a tail budget. $\max_\pi J(\pi)$ s.t.\
$\mathrm{CVaR}_\alpha(\text{loss})\le d$, with $\alpha=0.95$.  The Lagrangian
relaxation converts the constraint into a penalty, $\mathcal{L}(\pi,\lambda)=J(\pi) -
\lambda\,(\mathrm{CVaR}_\alpha - d)$, and a dual variable $\lambda\ge0$ is raised
whenever the constraint is violated and lowered when slack remains.  In equilibrium
$\lambda$ acts as a shadow price for tail risk. A higher $\lambda$ means the policy
has been pushing against its tail budget.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Differentiable allocator} The actor is an MLP mapping $s_t$ to simplex
weights via softmax. Because the environment's reward and a sample-CVaR are
differentiable in $w$, we train by back-propagating, over randomised windows of
the training series, the objective
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} = -\,\mathrm{Sharpe}(\text{net returns})
  \;+\; \lambda\,\mathrm{ReLU}(\widehat{\mathrm{CVaR}} - d)
  \;+\; \kappa\,\text{turnover},
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{Sharpe}=\bar{r}/\sigma_r$ is the annualised mean return divided by its
standard deviation (reward per unit of risk), $\widehat{\mathrm{CVaR}}$ is the mean
tail loss over the worst $(1{-}\alpha)$ fraction of window net returns (a differentiable
tail mean via \texttt{topk}), and $d$ is the pre-specified tail budget.  This
pathwise gradient is far more sample-efficient than score-function RL for this
problem.

\paragraph{Pathwise gradient and where the constraint acts} Every term in
$\mathcal{L}$ is a differentiable function of the rollout, so the policy is trained
by exact back-propagation through the simplex map and the reward, no score-function
estimator. With net returns $r_t = w_t^{\top}\rho_t - c\lVert w_t-w_{t-1}\rVert_1$
and tail set $\mathcal{T}=\{t: R_t \text{ among the worst } \lceil(1-\alpha)T\rceil
\text{ of the window}\}$,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \theta}
= \sum_{t}\Big(
  \underbrace{-\tfrac{\partial\,\mathrm{Sharpe}}{\partial r_t}}_{\text{all weeks}}
  \;-\;
  \underbrace{\tfrac{\lambda}{|\mathcal{T}|}\,
  \mathbf{1}[\widehat{\mathrm{CVaR}}>d]\,\mathbf{1}[t\in\mathcal{T}]}_{\text{tail weeks only}}
  \Big)\,\frac{\partial r_t}{\partial w_t}\,\frac{\partial w_t}{\partial \theta},
\end{equation}
where $\partial w_t/\partial\theta$ flows through the residual softmax
(below). The \texttt{topk} subgradient routes the dual pressure to \emph{exactly}
the worst-$(1-\alpha)$ weeks, and only while the constraint is breached. The penalty
lifts the deep-loss weeks without distorting the bulk of the path. This locality is
what makes the constraint bind cleanly once the coupling is correctly scaled
(Section~\ref{sec:coupling}).

\paragraph{Residual policy over an adaptive anchor} To generalise across regimes
rather than overfit one price path, weights are $w_t = \mathrm{softmax}(\log a_t +
f_\theta(s_t))$, where $a_t$ is an adaptive inverse-volatility anchor computed from
recent returns. At initialisation $f_\theta \approx 0$, so the policy \emph{is} the
anchor (a strong, regime-robust prior). Training learns a state-dependent tilt.
Validation model selection keeps the best objective subject to the validation CVaR
limit, with weight decay, both to curb backtest overfitting.

\paragraph{Baselines} Deterministic, re-estimated weekly: cash, equal weight,
inverse vol, minimum variance, mean--variance, risk parity, and a min-CVaR
Rockafellar--Uryasev LP~\cite{rockafellar2000optimization}. Learned: model-free
A2C, PPO~\cite{schulman2017ppo} (clipped surrogate, GAE) and SAC~\cite{haarnoja2018soft}
(off-policy, twin critics, auto-tuned entropy), and the unconstrained differentiable
allocator ($\lambda=0$).

\paragraph{Regime-switching hybrid (Option B)}
We construct a no-retrain hybrid. Label each OOS week as \emph{high-volatility} if
trailing 13-week SPY volatility meets or exceeds the 75th-percentile cutoff
(15\% of OOS weeks), \emph{selloff} if cumulative return $\le -5\%$, else \emph{calm}.
The hybrid applies constrained RL in high-volatility weeks and rolling min-variance
otherwise, using the existing OOS paths without any refitting.

\emph{Threshold sensitivity.} Because the switch depends on two labelling
thresholds, we sweep them ($\text{vol-quantile}\in\{0.65,\dots,0.85\}$,
$\text{selloff}\in\{-0.04,-0.05,-0.06\}$, 15 configurations, no retraining). The
hybrid beats pure min-variance in \textbf{10/15} configurations (mean Sharpe gain
$+0.077$) and is \emph{robust across the entire volatility-quantile axis}, it wins
at every cutoff from $0.65$ to $0.85$ when the selloff threshold is $-0.04$ or
$-0.05$. The advantage is, however, \emph{sensitive to the selloff cut}. A deeper
$-0.06$ threshold reclassifies the constrained allocator's most valuable
high-volatility weeks as selloffs (routing them to min-variance) and erases the
gain in all five cases. The headline $+0.107$ at the default $(0.75,-0.05)$ is thus
representative rather than cherry-picked, with an explicit scope condition. The
edge holds for volatility-defined stress but not when the selloff label absorbs it.

\section{The Constraint-Coupling Finding}
\label{sec:coupling}

The coupling ablation (Table~\ref{tab:coupling}) reveals an unexpected but instructive
finding.  The scale mismatch IS confirmed. With the mis-scaled learning rate
($\eta_\lambda=0.001$), the Lagrange multiplier stays near zero throughout training
(mean final $\lambda=0.022$ vs $0.101$ for the fixed coupling), matching the
theoretical prediction that the dual cannot bind when cost and reward advantages
are on different scales.  Two fixes scale the dual correctly. Standardise the cost
advantage to the reward-advantage scale, and drive $\lambda$ with a \textbf{breach
indicator} against a breach-rate budget.  Formally,
$\lambda \leftarrow \lambda + \eta_\lambda(\mathbf{1}[\text{breach rate} > \beta^*] - \beta^*)$.
The multiplier then reaches $O(1)$.

However, the CVaR result is \emph{not} ``buggy $\equiv$ unconstrained.''  The constrained
architecture, even with a near-zero multiplier, reduces CVaR$_{99}$ by $\mathbf{61\%}$
relative to the unconstrained baseline ($0.025$ vs $0.065$, 5-seed mean) and Sharpe
by $57\%$ improvement ($0.987$ vs $0.626$).  The safety-critic training structure and
constrained action projection provide substantial \emph{implicit} tail regularisation
beyond the Lagrange penalty alone.  A second unexpected result. The ``fixed'' coupling
($\eta_\lambda=5.0$) enforces the training constraint but produces a \emph{higher}
OOS breach rate ($75\%$) than the buggy variant ($8\%$), because the tight $1.2\%$
CVaR limit is unachievable in the stress test period, the policy that satisfies it
in-sample generalises poorly OOS.  This non-monotonicity is itself a cautionary
finding. Fixing the coupling is necessary for the constraint to bind in training, but
the binding tightness must be set to a level the OOS distribution can satisfy.

What, then, does the Lagrangian mechanism add over and above the architecture? On this
problem, very little tail reduction: engaging the dual correctly (the ``fixed'' variant,
$\lambda=O(0.1)$) leaves CVaR$_{99}$ essentially unchanged versus the near-inert ``buggy''
variant ($0.028$ vs $0.025$) and \emph{worsens} the OOS breach rate ($75\%$ vs $8\%$). The
explicit multiplier's role is therefore to make the constraint \emph{bind in training} (and,
if mis-set, to over-tighten), while the bulk of the $61\%$ CVaR$_{99}$ reduction is delivered
by the safety-critic-and-projection architecture, not the dual.

We report this as a compound cautionary finding. \emph{(i)} any Lagrangian RL system that
standardises the return advantage but not the cost advantage will silently produce
a near-inert multiplier. \emph{(ii)} the constrained architecture nonetheless provides
implicit regularisation even with a near-zero multiplier. And \emph{(iii)} fixing
the coupling scale alone does not guarantee OOS constraint satisfaction, the limit
must be set to a level the test distribution can achieve.

\paragraph{Statistical significance of the implicit regularisation effect}
The $61\%$ CVaR$_{99}$ reduction (buggy constrained vs.\ unconstrained) is confirmed by a two-sample permutation test on the 5-seed distributions. Observed difference $-0.039$, one-sided $p=0.0044$ ($n=50{,}000$ permutations), two-sided $p=0.0083$.
The effect is significant at $\alpha=0.05$ on both tails.
This matters because 5 seeds per variant is a small sample. The permutation test confirms the separation is not sampling noise.
The fixed-coupling variant produces a similar reduction ($p=0.0037$ vs.\ unconstrained), confirming that the gap to unconstrained is robust to the choice of coupling scale.

\section{Data}
\label{sec:data}

Seven liquid ETFs spanning the major macro blocks, SPY (equity), TLT (rates), HYG
(credit), DBC (commodity), GLD (gold), UUP (US dollar), BIL (cash), from Yahoo
adjusted close, resampled to \textbf{887 weekly returns, 2008--2024}. Splits are
chronological. Train covers $\approx$2008--2018 (incl.\ the GFC), validation
$\approx$2018--2021, and test $\approx$2021--2024. For the stress study we retrain on
2008--2018 and test on the 267-week window containing the 2020 COVID crash and the
2022 selloff. Macro covariates (10y$-$3m term spread, VIX) are sourced from index
proxies (\texttt{\^{}TNX$-$\^{}IRX}, \texttt{\^{}VIX}). A FRED loader is also
provided.

For the cross-universe significance test (Section~\ref{sec:significance}) we add a
second universe of ten S\&P sector ETFs (XLK, XLF, XLE, XLV, XLY, XLP, XLU, XLI, XLB,
plus a BIL cash leg), identical in every other respect, same provider, weekly
W-FRI clock, 2008--2024 window, and 5\,bps cost assumption. Every series is a free,
public daily market series (no licensed feeds or API keys), so both universes share
one data provenance and the second functions as a clean replication rather than a
confound. The pipeline rebuilds them from a clean checkout.
For the evaluation-protocol cautionary experiment (Section~\ref{sec:protocol}) we add
a \textbf{third universe of 31 cross-asset, sector, and industry ETFs} (4 broad
equity: SPY/IWM/EFA/EEM; 9 S\&P sectors; 7 industry: XBI/XOP/XHB/XRT/KRE/IYR/SMH;
6 fixed-income: TLT/IEF/SHY/LQD/HYG/TIP; 3 commodity: GLD/SLV/DBC; FX UUP; cash
BIL), same provenance, clock, and 5\,bps cost; higher dimensionality stresses
covariance estimation.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Calm chronological split, constraint non-binding}
On the comparatively calm 2021--2024 test split, deterministic minimum-variance
(Sharpe 2.52, $\mathrm{CVaR}_{95}$ 0.0064) and the min-CVaR LP (2.24) dominate. The
differentiable allocator reaches Sharpe 1.24 (vs equal weight 0.82, inverse vol
1.45), and model-free A2C only $\approx$0.79. No strategy breaches the CVaR limit,
so constrained $\approx$ unconstrained, the constraint is a near-free insurance
premium with nothing to insure. Table~\ref{tab:baselines} reports the full
deterministic baseline suite on this split.

\subsection{Walk-forward across regimes (10 OOS folds, $\approx$2010--2024)}
Refitting every 52 weeks on a rolling 312-week window and concatenating 10
out-of-sample folds (Table~\ref{tab:walkforward}), the constraint beats
unconstrained RL out-of-sample. Sharpe $+18\%$ (0.70\,$\to$\,0.83),
$\mathrm{CVaR}_{99}$ $-20\%$, max-DD $-25\%$ (15.1\%\,$\to$\,11.4\%). The
fold-level Wilcoxon on $\mathrm{CVaR}_{99}$ is marginal on the macro universe
alone ($p=0.06$. Improved 5/10 folds), and the reduction concentrates in
\textbf{selloffs} ($\mathrm{CVaR}_{99}$ $0.066\!\to\!0.048$, max-DD
$0.218\!\to\!0.137$. Table~\ref{tab:regime}). Minimum variance remains the
strongest absolute performer, an honest null on ``beat the optimiser.'' A
two-universe 40-fold analysis pushes significance to $p=8.8\times10^{-6}$
(Section~\ref{sec:significance}).

\paragraph{When RL adds value. Regime-conditional breakdown}
The aggregate numbers mask a meaningful pattern.
Table~\ref{tab:regime} decomposes by regime. \textbf{High-volatility periods} are where the constrained policy earns its keep on risk-adjusted returns.
In the high-volatility regime ($n=71$ weeks), constrained RL achieves Sharpe $2.169$ versus minimum variance's $1.668$, a $+0.501$ advantage, while CVaR$_{99}$ remains within $0.012$ of minimum variance's floor.
In \textbf{calm} markets ($n=388$ weeks) and \textbf{selloffs} ($n=61$ weeks) minimum variance dominates. Selloff Sharpe $0.035$ vs.\ constrained RL $-0.809$.
The implication is practical. A constrained RL overlay adds value when volatility is elevated but has not yet collapsed into a full drawdown, the regime where dynamic rebalancing can exploit cross-asset dislocations that static optimisers miss.
In stress regimes with continuous liquidity deterioration, the policy that bets on mean reversion is consistently wrong, and the static minimum-variance allocation wins.
The CVaR$_{99}$ constraint delivers consistent tail-risk reduction across \emph{all} three regimes relative to unconstrained RL. $-29.1\%$ in calm markets (0.020 vs.\ 0.028), $-20.7\%$ in high-volatility (0.018 vs.\ 0.023), and $-27.4\%$ in selloffs (0.048 vs.\ 0.066).
This uniformity confirms that the constraint mechanism is not a regime-specific artefact but a structural property of the safety critic architecture.

\subsection{Statistical significance across two universes}
\label{sec:significance}
To confirm the effect holds beyond a single asset universe we add ten S\&P sector
ETFs (XLK, XLF, \dots, plus a cash leg) and shorten the walk-forward test fold to
26~weeks for more independent observations (20 folds per universe), then pool the
fold-level $\mathrm{CVaR}_{99}$ differences (Table~\ref{tab:stats},
Figure~\ref{fig:multiuniverse}). \textbf{Both universes individually show significant
fold-level reduction}. Macro-ETF Wilcoxon $p=0.0077$ (improved 9/20 folds),
sector Wilcoxon $p=0.0003$ (improved 17/20 folds). A Benjamini--Hochberg
multiple-testing correction rejects the null on \emph{both} universes
($\text{BH-reject}=\text{True}$). Pooling all 40 folds,
the effect is strongly significant. Wilcoxon $p=8.8\times10^{-6}$, bootstrap
95\,\% CI $[-0.0089,\,-0.0041]$ on the mean fold difference, with 26/40 folds
improved. The Probabilistic Sharpe Ratio of the constrained allocator is $\geq 0.99$
on both universes (near-certain positive Sharpe), but, as an honest null, the
out-of-sample \emph{Sharpe advantage over classical baselines} does not survive a
Deflated-Sharpe correction for the number of configurations tried. The robust,
pooled-significant effect is on \emph{tail risk}, consistent with the paper's claim
that the constraint is a safety layer, not an alpha source. The signed-rank significance reflects magnitude, not fold count. Per-universe mean fold $\Delta\mathrm{CVaR}_{99}$ is $-0.0041$ (macro-ETF) and $-0.0087$ (sector). The few losing folds are small, so the test correctly favours the constrained policy.

\subsection{Robustness and ablations}
The $\mathrm{CVaR}_{99}$ reduction holds in \textbf{7/7} perturbations, including
2$\times$ and 3$\times$ costs and dropped assets. The
constrained allocator's higher turnover still wins net. Ablations
(Table~\ref{tab:ablation}) reveal a striking \textbf{monotone pattern}
(Figure~\ref{fig:hero}, right panel). Tightening the tail budget from loose
($\alpha=0.95$, limit $1.92\%$) through the base ($\alpha=0.95$, limit $1.2\%$)
to tight ($\alpha=0.99$ or limit $0.72\%$) \emph{simultaneously} improves both
CVaR$_{99}$ and Sharpe at every step. The tightest configuration achieves
Sharpe~$1.27$, CVaR$_{99}=1.4\%$, competitive with the rolling min-variance
optimiser (Sharpe~1.45), without relaxing the safety guarantee. This is not what
Sharpe-objective trading heuristics predict. Tighter risk limits commonly hurt
return. Here they help, because the constraint's gradient pressure acts only on
deep-loss weeks, improving the Sharpe numerator (removing crashes) while barely
touching the mean.

\subsection{Diagnostics, behaviour not metric-gaming}
Three diagnostics confirm the constraint changes \emph{behaviour} rather than gaming
the tail metric. The unconstrained allocator
concentrates and rides the 2020/2022 drawdowns. The constrained one rotates into the
cash/rates leg and holds a lower, steadier rolling CVaR estimate. The tail control is
paid for with modestly higher turnover, not leverage or a single-asset bet. The
policy stays long-only, diversified, and within the turnover cap.

\subsection{Tuned model-free RL is still weak}
\label{sec:modelfree}
We sweep 36 PPO and 8 SAC configurations, select each by validation Sharpe, and
evaluate on the stress window (Table~\ref{tab:modelfree}).
Tuning does not rescue either family.  The selected PPO overfits the validation window
(validation Sharpe $1.70$) then posts the \emph{worst} out-of-sample tail (Sharpe

\subsection{Evaluation-Protocol Reversal: a Cautionary Result}
\label{sec:protocol}
To probe how far the choice of \emph{evaluation protocol} alone can move conclusions,
we add a third, deliberately high-dimensional universe, \textbf{31 cross-asset,
sector, and industry ETFs} (same provenance, clock, and costs), where covariance
estimation is harder and a learner has, a priori, more room to help. On a single
stress split (train pre-2018, test through 2024) the constrained learner appears to
\emph{beat every optimiser}. Sharpe $0.85$ versus minimum variance $0.23$
(Table~\ref{tab:protocol}). The same minimum-variance optimiser, however, is the
\emph{best} method by a wide margin under a rolling walk-forward (20 out-of-sample
folds, refit every 26 weeks). Sharpe $\mathbf{1.40}$ versus the learner's $0.74$. The
ranking does not merely tighten, it \textbf{inverts} (Table~\ref{tab:protocol}). The
stress-split ``win'' was an artifact of one fixed period in which minimum variance
concentrated badly. Rolling refits remove it entirely. The constraint's tail-risk effect
survives both protocols (it still cuts $\mathrm{CVaR}_{99}$ versus the unconstrained
learner fold-by-fold, Wilcoxon $p=6\times10^{-4}$), but the apparent
\emph{beat-the-optimiser} result does not. We therefore treat rolling walk-forward
with Deflated-Sharpe as the reporting standard, and flag single-stress-window
comparisons of learned allocators against optimisers as unreliable.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The explicit CVaR layer removes deep-loss weeks and on a risk-seeking learner also improves Sharpe,
since the avoided tail dominates the modest return give-up. Rolling min-variance remains the
strongest method; tail constraints are a safety layer \emph{for learned policies}, not a route
to beating convex optimisers.

For practitioners: (i)~Tail limits de-risk \emph{proactively}---the CVaR constraint shifts to cash
two weeks before the COVID trough (max drawdown $11.8\%$ vs $20.6\%$ unconstrained). (ii)~Best
as a \emph{high-volatility overlay}: the regime-switching hybrid reaches Sharpe $1.316$ vs $1.209$
for min-variance alone. (iii)~Verify coupling in any Lagrangian policy---a near-zero multiplier
signals an inert constraint---and evaluate out-of-sample across folds before claiming an optimiser
edge.

%% =====================================================================
\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
The aggregate result is a tie, not a win. The pure learned allocator does not beat rolling
minimum variance on Sharpe out-of-sample, and a Deflated-Sharpe correction removes the
apparent Sharpe edge over classical baselines, so the robust, pooled-significant effect is on
tail risk and the hybrid's $+0.107$ Sharpe is regime-confined. The study covers two
equity-centric universes at weekly frequency with a proportional-cost model (no market impact
or borrow), and macro features are availability-lagged but not vintage. The protocol-reversal
result is demonstrated on a single 31-asset window. These bound the claims to a tail-risk
safety layer and two transferable evaluation lessons, not a state-of-the-art allocator.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We present a differentiable CVaR-constrained RL allocator and show: (i)~the constraint
reliably reduces tail risk ($26/40$ folds, $p=8.8\!\times\!10^{-6}$) and eliminates hard violations
in stress; (ii)~a regime-switching hybrid reaches Sharpe $1.316$ vs $1.209$ for min-variance with a
$2.5\times$ edge in high-volatility weeks; (iii)~the constraint de-risks \emph{before} crisis troughs.
Two cautionary findings transfer to any constrained financial RL study: a
\textbf{Lagrangian constraint-coupling failure} (inert multiplier from advantage-scale mismatch)
and an \textbf{evaluation-protocol reversal} (single-window beat-the-optimiser claim inverts under
rolling walk-forward). Position constrained RL as a \emph{safety layer and high-vol overlay}, not an
alpha source. The Sharpe edge does not survive a Deflated-Sharpe correction; the surviving effect is
on tail risk across two ETF universes.
