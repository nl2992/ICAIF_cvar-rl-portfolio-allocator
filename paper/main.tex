\begin{abstract}
Two failure modes leave Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR)-constrained financial
reinforcement learning (RL) unenforced without any visible symptom. A \textbf{Lagrangian
constraint-coupling failure}: standardising the reward advantage but not the cost advantage
leaves the dual permanently inert, so the CVaR budget is nominal rather than enforced. And an
\textbf{evaluation-protocol reversal}: on a 31-asset universe a single stress window ranks a
learned allocator above every classical optimiser, while a 20-fold rolling walk-forward
inverts that ranking (Wilcoxon $p=6\times10^{-4}$), so ``beat-the-optimiser'' claims can be
artefacts of the evaluation protocol rather than the policy. We name, diagnose, and resolve both on
a CVaR-constrained portfolio allocator (seven-ETF macro universe, 2008--2024,
weekly, 5\,bps costs, 40-fold walk-forward, pooled Wilcoxon $p=8.8\times10^{-6}$,
Benjamini--Hochberg (BH) and Deflated-Sharpe controlled). Scaling the dual correctly makes it
bind in training, but a budget that binds in training can still be breached out-of-sample
($71$--$78\%$ across four validation-calibrated rules): a second, budget-feasibility knob. The
fix is a decision-time projection refusing non-compliant trades: estimating forward CVaR
from trailing statistics and shrinking toward a trailing minimum-variance anchor cuts
out-of-sample breach to $7.4$--$7.9\%$, matching the classical optimisers' own compliance
rate, at Sharpe $0.85$--$0.99$ against rolling minimum-variance's $0.90$. Our claim is
tail-risk control rather than alpha, and it holds under multiplicity control. The
practical checklist is to standardise both advantage terms, verify the multiplier moves off zero, treat
budget feasibility as a separate knob, and enforce compliance at decision time.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
%% Context -> problem -> contributions -> scope/roadmap.

In the week of 16 March 2020, the S\&P 500 fell another 15\% in four days as
COVID-19 lockdowns shut economies worldwide.  A learned portfolio allocator trained
on the preceding decade, with no explicit rule about how much loss it was budgeted
to absorb, had no mechanical reason to rotate into Treasuries or cash when the VIX
tripled overnight.  Absent a tail limit, the policy kept holding equities through
the worst losses.  An otherwise-identical allocator carrying an explicit
Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR) limit had already rotated into the cash/rates leg
before the trough and emerged with roughly half the drawdown (11.8\% peak-to-trough
versus 20.6\% for its unconstrained twin, Table~\ref{tab:stress}).

That difference is a result, not the headline. An explicit CVaR constraint is a
safety layer that cuts tail losses of a \emph{learned} allocator without
sacrificing risk-adjusted return. In the first model-free implementation we tried,
a standard Lagrangian actor--critic, the \emph{dual channel was inert}: the
Lagrange multiplier stayed pinned near zero, so the constraint exerted no explicit
tail pressure through the mechanism meant to enforce it, a subtle scale mismatch we
describe, name, and diagnose in Section~\ref{sec:coupling}, likely present in any
Lagrangian RL system that standardises the return advantage but not the cost
advantage. As that section shows, the constrained \emph{architecture} still
regularises the tail through its safety-critic and projection structure even when
the multiplier is inert: the Lagrangian mechanism itself never engages.

Reinforcement learning for portfolio allocation is usually evaluated on return
or Sharpe ratio, with risk handled implicitly.  Practitioners, however, manage
\emph{tail} risk explicitly.  Markowitz mean--variance
optimisation~\cite{markowitz1952portfolio} penalises upside and downside
symmetrically and is silent on path-wise drawdown. CVaR is a coherent, convex tail
measure~\cite{rockafellar2000optimization} that RL can optimise directly.  We ask
a narrow, falsifiable question.

\begin{quote}
\emph{Does an explicit CVaR constraint reduce the downside tail losses and
constraint breaches of a learned allocator, versus an otherwise identical
unconstrained learner, while remaining competitive with standard portfolio
optimisers after costs?}
\end{quote}

\noindent\textbf{We make the following contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Two named failure modes in CVaR-constrained financial RL, with diagnostics
and, where demonstrated, fixes.} A \emph{Lagrangian constraint-coupling failure}: in the
model-free Lagrangian actor--critic, standardising the reward advantage but not the cost
advantage leaves the multiplier inert for any reasonable coupling scale, specific to
portfolio allocation, where returns are $O(10^{-3})$ and breach indicators are $O(1)$
(Section~\ref{sec:coupling}). An \emph{evaluation-protocol reversal}: on a 31-asset
universe a single stress window ranks the learned allocator above every optimiser, while a
rolling walk-forward inverts that ranking, so ``beat-the-optimiser'' results can be
protocol artefacts (Section~\ref{sec:protocol}).
\item \textbf{A differentiable CVaR-constrained allocator whose tail effect survives
multiplicity control.} On a leak-free, tested portfolio-control environment, a return
objective plus a differentiable CVaR penalty is back-propagated through the rollout, with a
Lagrangian dual on a breach-rate budget and a residual policy over an adaptive anchor
(Section~\ref{sec:method}). The tail-risk reduction holds on a 40-fold, two-universe
walk-forward under Benjamini--Hochberg and Deflated-Sharpe control
(Section~\ref{sec:significance}), and a regime-switching hybrid converts it into a
walk-forward Sharpe gain of $1.316$ vs $1.209$ for pure min-variance
(Sections~\ref{sec:results},~\ref{sec:discussion}).
\item \textbf{An evaluation protocol built to expose exactly this kind of artefact.}
Deterministic optimisers plus three tuned model-free RL families are compared under a
two-universe rolling walk-forward with Wilcoxon, Benjamini--Hochberg, and Deflated-Sharpe
control, the design that surfaces the protocol reversal in Contribution 1 rather than
concealing it. The regime-switching hybrid is a supporting observation within this
protocol, not a separate contribution.
\end{enumerate}

We are explicit about scope. The pure learned allocator does \textbf{not} beat a rolling
minimum-variance optimiser in aggregate, and we do not claim it should: the \emph{constrained}
allocator's value is tail control, and the regime-switching hybrid converts that into an
out-of-sample Sharpe gain ($+0.107$) in the high-volatility regime where the tail constraint
is most active. The broader contribution is a differentiable constraint mechanism that
demonstrably binds and cuts tails, together with two transferable failure modes that any
constrained-RL-for-finance study must control for or risk reporting an artefact. The
allocator is the testbed that makes both visible.

\section{Background and related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Mean--variance optimisation and its inputs} Mean--variance optimisation
(MVO)~\cite{markowitz1952portfolio} is sensitive to input estimates. Decision-focused
learning trains return predictors to improve downstream MVO decisions rather than raw forecast
accuracy~\cite{lee2024dfl}, and mean--variance-efficient collaborative filtering
blends user preference with portfolio theory~\cite{chung2023mvecf}. Both improve the
\emph{inputs} to a variance-based objective. We instead constrain a \emph{tail} risk
measure on a learned policy.

\paragraph{Conditional Value-at-Risk} $\mathrm{CVaR}_\alpha$ is the expected
portfolio loss on the worst $(1{-}\alpha)$ fraction of outcomes, the average of losses
exceeding the $\alpha$-quantile ($\mathrm{VaR}_\alpha$). Unlike variance it ignores
upside, unlike $\mathrm{VaR}$ it averages across the whole tail. Rockafellar and
Uryasev~\cite{rockafellar2000optimization} give the tractable form
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{CVaR}_\alpha(L) \;=\; \min_{\zeta\in\mathbb{R}}\!\left\{
    \zeta + \frac{1}{1-\alpha}\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\max(L-\zeta,\,0)\right]
  \right\},
\end{equation}
where $L$ denotes the portfolio loss and the inner expectation is over the empirical
loss distribution.  The minimum is achieved at $\zeta=\mathrm{VaR}_\alpha$, the
representation admits a convex linear program (LP, we include this as a baseline) and a
differentiable penalty via \texttt{topk}.

\paragraph{RL for portfolio management}
The dominant pattern in RL-for-portfolio literature is to train on a single historical
window, evaluate on one held-out test split, and compare against equal-weight or
buy-and-hold. EIIE~\cite{jiang2017deep} compares twelve classical strategies on one
$\approx$50-day crypto window, and SARL~\cite{ye2020sarl} reports higher return on a fixed
period without cross-validation, and FinRL~\cite{liu2021finrl} supports rolling retraining
but demonstrates on a single split with no significance tests,
Huang~\cite{huang2025ctrl} applies continuous-time RL to asset--liability management
but targets a quadratic surplus objective, not a tail constraint. None uses CVaR as a
constraint, seeds
multiple runs, or applies statistical testing. We are not aware of prior work
subjecting RL portfolio allocation to a 40-fold, two-universe walk-forward with
Wilcoxon and Benjamini--Hochberg control, the protocol that exposes the reversal
artefact.

\paragraph{Direct reinforcement and pathwise gradients}
Moody and Saffell~\cite{moody2001learning} first trained a recurrent trading policy by
back-propagating the \emph{differential Sharpe ratio} directly through the return
function. Buehler et al.~\cite{buehler2019deephedging} (Deep Hedging) extend this to
neural derivative hedging, back-propagating CVaR as a risk-aversion \emph{utility}
through a simulated trajectory. Our allocator inherits the pathwise-gradient insight
from both but replaces the risk-aversion coefficient with an explicit CVaR
\emph{budget constraint} enforced by a dual variable. Model-free policy gradients
(Advantage Actor-Critic, A2C, and Proximal Policy Optimization,
PPO~\cite{schulman2017ppo}) and off-policy actor--critics
(Soft Actor-Critic, SAC~\cite{haarnoja2018soft}) ignore this differentiable structure. Our allocator
exploits it, decisive at this sample size (887 weekly observations,
Section~\ref{sec:modelfree}). ProtoHedge~\cite{faloughi2025protohedge} similarly makes
such policies auditable via a prototype-similarity policy.

\paragraph{CVaR-constrained RL}
Chow et al.~\cite{chow2018riskconstrained} derive the Lagrangian gradient for CVaR-constrained
CMDPs. Tessler et al.~\cite{tessler2019rcpo} (RCPO) and Ying et al.~\cite{ying2022cppo} (CPPO)
implement hard CVaR constraints for robotics control tasks. None identifies the
\emph{constraint-coupling failure} we describe. Standardising return-advantage to unit
scale without normalising the cost advantage makes the Lagrange multiplier permanently
inert, a failure specific to portfolio allocation where returns are $O(10^{-3})$ and
breach indicators are $O(1)$, invisible in robotics. We pose allocation as a
constrained MDP~\cite{achiam2017cpo} and show the well-scaled dual is what makes the
constraint bind. The closest concurrent work is \citet{dong2025clauselens}
(\textsc{ClauseLens}), a CVaR-constrained RL system for reinsurance pricing. Our
contribution is orthogonal, naming failure modes transferable to any CVaR-constrained
financial RL system.

\section{Problem formulation}
\label{sec:formulation}

\paragraph{Markov Decision Process (MDP)} At weekly step $t$ the agent observes state $s_t$ (per-asset
26-week momentum and volatility, current weights, drawdown, a rolling 95\% CVaR
estimate, and optional exogenous macro/factor features) and chooses post-trade
weights $w_t$ on the long-only simplex. The environment projects $w_t$ onto the
admissible set $\mathcal{W}$ (max weight 0.40, turnover cap 0.50, optional cash
floor), charges proportional cost $c\lVert w_t - w_{t-1}\rVert_1$ ($c=5$\,bps), and
realises $r_t = w_t^{\top}\rho_t - c\lVert w_t - w_{t-1}\rVert_1$, where $\rho_t$
are next-week asset returns. Features at $t$ use only $\rho_{<t}$ (no look-ahead,
enforced by tests).

\paragraph{Constrained objective (CMDP)} A \emph{constrained Markov Decision
Process} (CMDP)~\cite{achiam2017cpo} augments the standard MDP with one or more
inequality constraints that the policy must satisfy on average.  We maximise
risk-adjusted return subject to a tail budget. $\max_\pi J(\pi)$ s.t.\
$\mathrm{CVaR}_\alpha(\text{loss})\le d$, with $\alpha=0.95$.  The Lagrangian
relaxation converts the constraint into a penalty, $\mathcal{L}(\pi,\lambda)=J(\pi) -
\lambda\,(\mathrm{CVaR}_\alpha - d)$, and a dual variable $\lambda\ge0$ is raised
whenever the constraint is violated and lowered when slack remains.  In equilibrium
$\lambda$ acts as a shadow price for tail risk. A higher $\lambda$ means the policy
has been pushing against its tail budget.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Differentiable allocator} The actor is an MLP mapping $s_t$ to simplex
weights via softmax. Because the environment's reward and a sample-CVaR are
differentiable in $w$, we train by back-propagating, over randomised windows of
the training series, the objective
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} = -\,\mathrm{Sharpe}(\text{net returns})
  \;+\; \lambda\,\mathrm{ReLU}(\widehat{\mathrm{CVaR}} - d)
  \;+\; \kappa\,\text{turnover},
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{Sharpe}=\bar{r}/\sigma_r$ is the annualised mean return divided by its
standard deviation (reward per unit of risk), $\widehat{\mathrm{CVaR}}$ is the mean
tail loss over the worst $(1{-}\alpha)$ fraction of window net returns (a differentiable
tail mean via \texttt{topk}), and $d$ is the pre-specified tail budget.  This
pathwise gradient is far more sample-efficient than score-function RL for this
problem.

\paragraph{Pathwise gradient and where the constraint acts} Every term in
$\mathcal{L}$ is a differentiable function of the rollout, so the policy is trained
by exact back-propagation through the simplex map and the reward, no score-function
estimator. With net returns $r_t = w_t^{\top}\rho_t - c\lVert w_t-w_{t-1}\rVert_1$
and tail set $\mathcal{T}=\{t: R_t \text{ among the worst } \lceil(1-\alpha)T\rceil
\text{ of the window}\}$,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \theta}
= \sum_{t}\Big(
  \underbrace{-\tfrac{\partial\,\mathrm{Sharpe}}{\partial r_t}}_{\text{all weeks}}
  \;-\;
  \underbrace{\tfrac{\lambda}{|\mathcal{T}|}\,
  \mathbf{1}[\widehat{\mathrm{CVaR}}>d]\,\mathbf{1}[t\in\mathcal{T}]}_{\text{tail weeks only}}
  \Big)\,\frac{\partial r_t}{\partial w_t}\,\frac{\partial w_t}{\partial \theta},
\end{equation}
where $\partial w_t/\partial\theta$ flows through the residual softmax
(below). The \texttt{topk} subgradient routes the dual pressure to \emph{exactly}
the worst-$(1-\alpha)$ weeks, and only while the constraint is breached. The penalty
lifts the deep-loss weeks without distorting the bulk of the path. This locality is
what makes the constraint bind cleanly once the coupling is correctly scaled
(Section~\ref{sec:coupling}).

\paragraph{Residual policy over an adaptive anchor} To generalise across regimes
rather than overfit one price path, weights are $w_t = \mathrm{softmax}(\log a_t +
f_\theta(s_t))$, where $a_t$ is an adaptive inverse-volatility anchor computed from
recent returns. At initialisation $f_\theta \approx 0$, so the policy \emph{is} the
anchor (a strong, regime-robust prior). Training learns a state-dependent tilt.
Validation model selection keeps the best objective subject to the validation CVaR
limit, with weight decay, both to curb backtest overfitting.

\paragraph{Baselines} Deterministic, re-estimated weekly: cash, equal weight,
inverse vol, minimum variance, mean--variance, risk parity, and a min-CVaR
Rockafellar--Uryasev LP~\cite{rockafellar2000optimization}. Learned: model-free A2C,
PPO~\cite{schulman2017ppo} (clipped surrogate, generalised advantage estimation, GAE), SAC~\cite{haarnoja2018soft}
(off-policy, twin critics, auto-tuned entropy), and the unconstrained differentiable allocator ($\lambda=0$).

\paragraph{Regime-switching hybrid (Option B)}
We construct a no-retrain hybrid: label each out-of-sample (OOS) week \emph{high-volatility} if
trailing 13-week SPY volatility meets the 75th-percentile cutoff (15\% of OOS weeks),
\emph{selloff} if cumulative return $\le -5\%$, else \emph{calm}. The hybrid applies
constrained RL in high-volatility weeks and rolling min-variance otherwise, reusing
the existing OOS paths without refitting.

\emph{Threshold sensitivity.} Because the switch depends on two labelling thresholds,
we sweep them ($\text{vol-quantile}\in\{0.65,\dots,0.85\}$, $\text{selloff}
\in\{-0.04,-0.05,-0.06\}$, 15 configurations, no retraining, Table~\ref{tab:threshold}).
The hybrid beats pure min-variance in \textbf{10/15} configurations (mean gain
$+0.077$), winning at every volatility cutoff when the selloff threshold is $-0.04$
or $-0.05$, but losing at all five cutoffs once the selloff threshold deepens to
$-0.06$, which reclassifies the constrained allocator's most valuable high-volatility
weeks as selloffs and routes them to min-variance instead. The headline $+0.107$ at
the default $(0.75,-0.05)$ is representative rather than cherry-picked: the edge
holds for volatility-defined stress but not once the selloff label absorbs it.

\section{Decomposing the constraint: dual, architecture, and budget feasibility}
\label{sec:coupling}

We designed a three-arm diagnostic to isolate what a Lagrangian CVaR constraint
contributes, and where it can fail silently. All three arms share the identical
architecture (actor, safety critic, admissible-set projection) and training budget
(5 seeds, 1500 updates). Only the dual's learning rate $\eta_\lambda$ and the
presence of the constraint differ (Table~\ref{tab:coupling}). The \textbf{mis-scaled
dual} ($\eta_\lambda=0.001$) standardises the reward advantage but leaves the cost
advantage unstandardised. The \textbf{scaled dual} ($\eta_\lambda=5.0$) standardises
both. The \textbf{unconstrained} arm removes the CVaR penalty entirely. Three named
findings follow.

\paragraph{Finding 1: dual inertness from an advantage-scale mismatch} With the
mis-scaled dual, the multiplier stays near zero throughout training (mean final
$\lambda=0.022$ vs.\ $0.101$ for the scaled dual): portfolio returns are $O(10^{-3})$
while the breach indicator that drives $\lambda$ is $O(1)$, so the unstandardised
cost advantage is too small, relative to the reward advantage, to move the
multiplier. Standardising the cost advantage to the reward-advantage scale, and
driving $\lambda$ with a breach indicator against a breach-rate budget,
$\lambda \leftarrow \lambda + \eta_\lambda(\mathbf{1}[\text{breach rate} > \beta^*] -
\beta^*)$, restores $\lambda$ to $O(0.1)$. The transferable diagnostic: multiplier
telemetry flat at zero across training means the constraint is decorative,
regardless of what the loss curve shows.

\paragraph{Finding 2: most of the tail control is architectural, not the dual}
With $\lambda$ pinned near zero, the constrained architecture alone, safety critic
plus projection, still cuts CVaR$_{99}$ by $\mathbf{61\%}$ against unconstrained
($0.025$ vs $0.065$, 5-seed mean) and raises Sharpe by $58\%$ ($0.987$ vs $0.626$),
confirmed by a permutation test on the 5-seed distributions (observed difference
$-0.039$, one-sided $p=0.0044$, $n=50{,}000$. Two-sided $p=0.0083$). The scaled-dual
arm produces a similar reduction against unconstrained ($p=0.0037$), so the gap to
unconstrained does not depend on the dual engaging. Engaging the dual correctly
($\lambda=O(0.1)$) leaves CVaR$_{99}$ essentially unchanged versus the near-inert
mis-scaled dual ($0.028$ vs $0.025$): most of the $61\%$ reduction is delivered by
the architecture, not the multiplier. This is a positive result in its own right: a
constrained architecture is a tail-risk regulariser on its own, not merely a
delivery mechanism for the Lagrangian penalty.

\paragraph{Finding 3: budget feasibility is a second knob, and it needs enforcement,
not another calibration} The scaled dual binds in training, but at the tight budget
($d=1.2\%$) produces a \emph{higher} OOS breach rate ($75\%$) than the mis-scaled dual
($8\%$), though classical rules clear the same budget in $92\%$ of weeks
(Section~\ref{sec:budget-reach}). Four pre-specified rules for recalibrating $d$ from
validation data (a loose $1.92\%$ budget, matching the policy's own or the anchor's
validation-realised $\mathrm{CVaR}_{0.95}$. A per-fold adaptive budget over a 10-fold
walk-forward) all land in a $71$--$78\%$ OOS breach band: a return-seeking Lagrangian
gives up compliance for return under tail shift whatever $d$ is, because the budget
was never the broken part.

\paragraph{The fix: enforce the budget, don't discourage its violation} We build the
mechanism the project design named but had not implemented: a decision-time CVaR
\emph{filter}. Between the actor's proposal and the environment we estimate forward
CVaR from a trailing covariance and mean only, both computed from returns strictly
before $t$ ($\mathrm{CVaR}\approx c_\alpha\sigma_p-\mu_p$, where $\mu_p$ and $\sigma_p$
are the trailing portfolio mean and volatility and $c_\alpha$ is the Gaussian
quantile at level $\alpha$, $c_{0.95}=2.06$), if it exceeds $d$ we
shrink the proposal along the line toward a trailing minimum-variance anchor, solving
numerically for the largest mixing weight that clears the budget, then trade the
shrunk weights, with turnover/max-weight/long-only composed after as before. A
look-ahead audit (\texttt{tests/test\_risk\_filter\_no\_lookahead.py}) confirms the
decision at $t$ is unchanged by shifting the return series and is a pure function of
trailing statistics. Both pass with zero deviation. We train with the filter active.

Four arms mirror the coupling arms exactly (5 seeds, 1500 updates, identical splits),
changing only the named quantities (Table~\ref{tab:coupling}, bottom block,
Figure~\ref{fig:frontier}). \textbf{A5, filter only} ($\eta_\lambda=0.001$) cuts breach
from $75.1\%$ to $7.9\%$, matching the best classical rules, at Sharpe $0.987$ against
minimum variance's $0.901$, and breaches less than half as deeply (mean excess
$0.0126$ vs.\ $0.0294$). \textbf{A6, filter + scaled dual} (our intended method) goes
\emph{below} every classical rule on breach rate ($7.4\%$), with
$\mathrm{CVaR}_{99}$ $0.016$ and mean excess $0.0043$, at Sharpe $0.851$.
\textbf{A7, adaptive dual} ($\lambda$ updated online from realised past losses, filter
off) is the negative control: breach stays at $81.9\%$ even as $\lambda$ climbs to a
mean of $7.69$, so test-time dual adaptation alone enforces nothing. It is the filter,
not the adaptation, that does the work. \textbf{A8} recovers most compliance
($14.6\%$) but less reliably, $7$--$33\%$ across seeds against zero seed-to-seed
variance for A5 and A6.

The filter pulls the policy toward the low-volatility region. A5's mean weight-space
$L_1$ distance to rolling minimum variance is $0.476$, correlation $0.786$, top
holding differing in $9.4\%$ of rebalances, against $0.890$, $0.201$ and $97.5\%$ for
the unfiltered scaled dual. The filtered policy is min-variance-\emph{adjacent}, and
it is not a redundant way to compute minimum variance: from that region it dominates
minimum variance on both axes at once, higher Sharpe at equal breach rate and half the
breach depth (A5), and a lower breach rate outright (A6). A soft Lagrangian cannot
enforce a tail budget out of sample. A decision-time projection can.

Refitting every 52 weeks on a rolling 312-week window and concatenating 10

unconstrained RL out-of-sample. Sharpe $+18\%$ (0.70\,$\to$\,0.83),

fold-level Wilcoxon on $\mathrm{CVaR}_{99}$ is marginal on the macro universe

\textbf{selloffs} ($\mathrm{CVaR}_{99}$ $0.066\!\to\!0.048$, max-DD

strongest absolute performer, an honest null on ``beat the optimiser.'' A
two-universe 40-fold analysis pushes significance to $p=8.8\times10^{-6}$
(Section~\ref{sec:significance}).

\paragraph{When RL adds value: regime-conditional breakdown}
\textbf{High-volatility} periods ($n{=}71$) are where the constrained policy earns

but has not yet collapsed into a full drawdown, not once liquidity deteriorates.

three} regimes ($-29.1\%$ calm, $-20.7\%$ high-vol, $-27.4\%$ selloff), a
structural property of the architecture rather than a regime-specific artefact.

\subsection{Statistical significance across two universes}
\label{sec:significance}
To confirm the effect holds beyond a single asset universe we add ten S\&P sector
ETFs (XLK, XLF, \dots, plus a cash leg) and shorten the walk-forward test fold to
26~weeks for more independent observations (20 folds per universe), then pool the
fold-level $\mathrm{CVaR}_{99}$ differences (Table~\ref{tab:stats}). \textbf{Both universes individually show significant
fold-level reduction}: macro-ETF Wilcoxon $p=0.0077$ (9/20 improved), sector
Wilcoxon $p=0.0003$ (17/20 improved), and Benjamini--Hochberg rejects the null on
\emph{both} ($\text{BH-reject}=\text{True}$). Pooling all 40 folds, Wilcoxon
$p=8.8\times10^{-6}$, bootstrap 95\,\% CI
$[-0.0089,\,-0.0041]$ on the mean fold difference, 26/40 folds improved. The
Probabilistic Sharpe Ratio of the constrained allocator is $\geq 0.99$ on both
universes. The out-of-sample \emph{Sharpe advantage over
classical baselines}, however, does not survive a Deflated-Sharpe correction, which discounts a
Sharpe ratio's apparent significance by the number of strategy configurations searched before
reporting it, controlling for selection bias from trying many variants. The pooled-significant
effect is on \emph{tail risk}, consistent with a safety layer rather than an alpha source. A weaker companion
statistic, disclosed for completeness: a pooled sign test on fold direction alone
gives 26/40, $p=0.081$, short of significance, because the Wilcoxon test weighs
each fold by improvement size, the losing folds are small and the winning folds
large, not merely more-win-than-lose. Per-universe mean fold
$\Delta\mathrm{CVaR}_{99}$ is $-0.0041$ (macro-ETF) and $-0.0087$ (sector),
consistent with that reading.

\subsection{Diagnostics, behaviour not metric-gaming}
Three diagnostics confirm the constraint changes \emph{behaviour} rather than gaming
the tail metric: the unconstrained allocator concentrates and rides the 2020/2022
drawdowns, the constrained one rotates into the cash/rates leg and holds a lower,
steadier rolling CVaR estimate, and the tail control is paid for with modestly
higher turnover, not leverage or a single-asset bet. The policy stays long-only,
diversified, and within the turnover cap.

\subsection{Evaluation-protocol reversal}
\label{sec:protocol}
To probe how far the choice of \emph{evaluation protocol} alone can move conclusions,
we add a third, high-dimensional universe, \textbf{31 cross-asset, sector, and
industry ETFs} (same provenance, clock, costs), where covariance estimation is
harder and a learner has, a priori, more room to help. On a single stress split
(train pre-2018, test through 2024) the constrained learner appears to \emph{beat
every optimiser}: Sharpe $0.85$ versus minimum variance $0.23$
(Table~\ref{tab:protocol}). The same minimum-variance optimiser is instead the
\emph{best} method by a wide margin under a rolling walk-forward (20 OOS folds,
refit every 26 weeks): Sharpe $\mathbf{1.40}$ versus the learner's $0.74$. The
ranking does not merely tighten, it \textbf{inverts}. The stress-split ``win'' was
an artefact of one fixed period in which minimum variance concentrated badly,
rolling refits remove it entirely. The constraint's tail-risk effect survives both
protocols (it still cuts $\mathrm{CVaR}_{99}$ versus the unconstrained learner
fold-by-fold, Wilcoxon $p=6\times10^{-4}$), but the apparent
\emph{beat-the-optimiser} result does not. We treat rolling walk-forward with
Deflated-Sharpe as the reporting standard, and flag single-stress-window
comparisons of learned allocators against optimisers as unreliable.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The explicit CVaR layer removes deep-loss weeks and, on a risk-seeking learner, also improves
Sharpe, since the avoided tail dominates the modest return give-up.

\paragraph{Is the learner just minimum variance under another name?} We measure this
rather than concede it, and the answer differs by arm. The \emph{unfiltered} learner is
far from it: mean per-rebalance $L_1$ weight distance to the rolling min-variance path
is $0.89$ (of a maximum $2.0$), correlation $0.20$, disagreeing on the top-weighted
asset in $97\%$ of weeks, so the resemblance the tightest ablation invites
(Figure~\ref{fig:hero}, right) is in \emph{aggregate risk statistics}, not holdings.
The decision-time filter deliberately moves the policy closer ($L_1$ $0.48$,
correlation $0.79$): compliance is bought partly by occupying the low-volatility
region. It does not merely reproduce minimum variance from there, but dominates it on
Sharpe and breach depth at once (Section~\ref{sec:coupling}).

For practitioners: (i)~tail limits de-risk \emph{proactively} (the CVaR constraint shifts to
cash two weeks before the COVID trough, max drawdown $11.8\%$ vs $20.6\%$ unconstrained),
(ii)~best used as a \emph{high-volatility overlay}, the regime-switching hybrid reaches Sharpe
$1.316$ vs $1.209$ for min-variance alone, (iii)~verify coupling in any Lagrangian policy, a
near-zero multiplier signals an inert constraint, and evaluate out-of-sample across folds before
claiming an optimiser edge.

\section{Scope}
\label{sec:limitations}
Our claims are deliberately scoped: the robust, multiplicity-controlled effect is
tail-risk reduction, not alpha. The pure learned allocator does not beat rolling
minimum variance on Sharpe out-of-sample, and a Deflated-Sharpe correction removes the
apparent Sharpe edge over classical baselines, so the hybrid's $+0.107$ gain is
regime-confined. The study covers two equity-centric universes at weekly frequency
with a proportional-cost model (no market impact or borrow), macro features are
availability-lagged but not vintage, and the protocol-reversal result is demonstrated
on a single 31-asset window. These bound the claims to a tail-risk safety layer and
transferable evaluation lessons, not a state-of-the-art allocator.

\section{Conclusion and future work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We present a differentiable CVaR-constrained RL allocator and show: (i)~the constraint
reliably reduces tail risk ($26/40$ folds, $p=8.8\!\times\!10^{-6}$) and eliminates hard
violations in stress, (ii)~a regime-switching hybrid reaches Sharpe $1.316$ vs $1.209$ for
min-variance, (iii)~the constraint de-risks \emph{before} crisis troughs. Two failure
modes transfer to any constrained financial RL study, a \textbf{Lagrangian
constraint-coupling failure} and an \textbf{evaluation-protocol reversal}. A decision-time
CVaR filter fixes what neither fix alone could, out-of-sample budget compliance.
Position constrained RL as a \emph{safety layer and high-vol overlay}: the surviving
effect is tail risk, and now compliance too, not alpha.
