\block{Abstract}{\body
  Reinforcement learning (RL) portfolio allocators are often given a tail-risk limit through a
  Lagrangian penalty on Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR). We show two ways in which this limit
  can fail with no visible symptom. When the penalty and the reward are on different scales,
  the penalty weight $\lambda$ never moves away from zero, yet tail metrics still improve
  because of other parts of the pipeline. And the choice of test decides how the learner ranks
  against classical optimisers. We add a filter that checks each portfolio before it trades and
  moves it toward minimum variance when its forecast CVaR exceeds the budget. Out of sample,
  the share of weeks over budget fell from \hl{75.1\% to 7.4\%}. Across 40 walk-forward folds
  on two ETF universes, the constraint lowered $\mathrm{CVaR}_{99}$ in \hl{26 of the 27} folds
  where it was active ($p=8.8\times10^{-6}$).
}

\block{1\quad Motivation}{\body
  \sidefig{0.5}{figure_stress_paths.pdf}{Figure 1. Drawdown over 2020 to 2024. Thick lines are
  five-seed means.}{In the week of 16 March 2020 the S\&P 500 fell about 15\% and the VIX
  closed at 82.69. An RL allocator trained only for return kept its equities. The same
  allocator trained with a CVaR budget had already moved into Treasuries and cash, and its
  drawdown was 7.7\% against 16.3\%. A budget of this kind is usually imposed with a
  Lagrangian penalty whose weight $\lambda$ grows while the portfolio is over budget [2].
  Section 4 asks whether $\lambda$ actually grows, and Section 5 asks whether the test used to
  judge the result can be trusted.}
}

\block{2\quad Problem and measures}{\body
  \sidefig{0.52}{figure_cvar_tail.pdf}{Figure 2. Weekly returns on the stress window, five
  seeds pooled. The shaded left tail is the worst 5\% of constrained weeks. Lines sit at
  minus each allocator's CVaR.}{$\mathrm{CVaR}_\alpha$ is the average loss in the worst
  $(1-\alpha)$ share of weeks [1]. With $\alpha=0.95$ it averages the worst 5\%, so it
  measures how large the losses are beyond the Value-at-Risk (VaR) threshold. Pooled over
  seeds, $\mathrm{CVaR}_{0.95}$ was 1.92\% with the constraint and 3.41\% without (per-seed
  means 1.89\% and 3.33\%).}
  Each week the allocator chooses long-only weights $w_t$ on seven ETFs (SPY, TLT, HYG, DBC,
  GLD, UUP, BIL), using 887 weekly returns from 2008 to 2024. The problem is a constrained
  Markov decision process [2],
  \[
    \max_\pi\ J(\pi)\quad\text{s.t.}\quad \mathrm{CVaR}_{0.95}(L)\le d=1.2\%,\qquad
    \mathrm{CVaR}_\alpha(L)=\min_{\zeta\in\mathbb{R}}\Big\{\zeta+\tfrac{1}{1-\alpha}\,
      \mathbb{E}\big[(L-\zeta)^{+}\big]\Big\}.
  \]
  All measures use weekly net returns $r_1,\dots,r_T$ and wealth $W_t=\prod_{u\le t}(1+r_u)$,
  \begin{align*}
    \mathrm{Sharpe}&=\sqrt{52}\;\bar r/s_r, &
    \mathrm{MDD}&=\max_t\big(1-W_t/\textstyle\max_{u\le t}W_u\big),\\
    \widehat{\mathrm{CVaR}}_\alpha(r)&=-\operatorname{mean}\{r_t:\ r_t\le\hat q_{1-\alpha}(r)\}, &
    B&=\tfrac{1}{|\mathcal{T}|}\textstyle\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}}
      \mathbf{1}\big[\widehat{\mathrm{CVaR}}_{0.95}(r_{t-52:t-1})>d\big],
  \end{align*}
  where $\hat q_{1-\alpha}$ is the empirical quantile and $B$ is the breach rate over the test
  weeks $\mathcal{T}$, of which 5\% may breach. Sharpe significance uses the Probabilistic and
  Deflated Sharpe ratios [4], with per-week $\widehat{SR}$, skewness $\hat\gamma_3$, kurtosis
  $\hat\gamma_4$ and $N$ configurations tried,
  \[
    \mathrm{PSR}(SR^\ast)=\Phi\!\left(\frac{(\widehat{SR}-SR^\ast)\sqrt{T-1}}
      {\sqrt{1-\hat\gamma_3\widehat{SR}+\frac{\hat\gamma_4-1}{4}\widehat{SR}^{2}}}\right),\qquad
    SR^\ast_{\mathrm{DSR}}=\hat\sigma_{SR}\Big[(1-\gamma)\Phi^{-1}\big(1-\tfrac1N\big)
      +\gamma\,\Phi^{-1}\big(1-\tfrac{1}{Ne}\big)\Big],
  \]
  where $\gamma$ is the Euler-Mascheroni constant.
}

\block{3\quad Method}{\body
  The allocator acts once a week. Figure 3 writes the decision as a chain of maps, and the four
  steps below define each one.
  \par\medskip\centering
  \begin{tikzpicture}[
      v/.style={font=\fontsize{30}{34}\selectfont, inner sep=2mm},
      def/.style={font=\fontsize{18.5}{22}\selectfont, text=dark, align=center, text width=74mm},
      map/.style={-{Latex[length=6mm, width=6mm]}, line width=1.2mm, color=dark},
      lab/.style={above, font=\fontsize{21}{24}\selectfont, text=ink},
      node distance=0mm]
    \node[v] (s) at (0,0) {$s_t$};
    \node[v] (wt) at (7.35,0) {$\tilde w_t$};
    \node[v] (wh) at (15.1,0) {$\hat w_t$};
    \node[v] (w) at (22.7,0) {$w_t$};
    \node[v] (r) at (29.7,0) {$r_t$};
    \draw[map] (s) -- node[lab] {(i) $f_\theta$} (wt);
    \draw[map, color=navy, line width=1.8mm] (wt) -- node[lab, text=navy] {(ii) filter $\Phi_d$} (wh);
    \draw[map] (wh) -- node[lab] {(iii) $\Pi_{\mathcal W}$} (w);
    \draw[map] (w) -- node[lab] {market} (r);
    \node[def, below=5mm of s] {state: momentum,\\ volatility, weights,\\ drawdown, CVaR};
    \node[def, below=5mm of wt] {$\mathrm{softmax}(\log a_t+f_\theta(s_t))$};
    \node[def, below=5mm of wh, text=navy] {$s^\ast\tilde w_t+(1-s^\ast)\,w^{\mathrm{MV}}_t$};
    \node[def, below=5mm of w] {$w\ge0,\ w_i\le0.4$,\\ $\lVert w-w_{t-1}\rVert_1\le0.5$};
    \node[def, below=5mm of r] {$w_t^{\top}\rho_t-c\lVert w_t-w_{t-1}\rVert_1$};
    \draw[map, dashed, color=mid] (r.north) -- ++(0,26mm) -| node[pos=0.25, above,
      font=\fontsize{19}{22}\selectfont, text=dark]
      {(iv) training: $\nabla_\theta\mathcal{L}(\theta,\lambda)$ through the rollout}
      ($(s)!0.5!(wt)+(0,14mm)$);
  \end{tikzpicture}
  \par\figcap{Figure 3. The decision at week $t$ as a composition of maps.}
  \RaggedRight
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*), leftmargin=1.6em, itemsep=2mm]
    \item \emph{Policy.} An MLP $f_\theta$ tilts an adaptive inverse-volatility anchor $a_t$,
      $\tilde w_t=\mathrm{softmax}(\log a_t+f_\theta(s_t))$. At initialisation
      $f_\theta\approx0$, so training starts from the anchor and learns a state-dependent
      tilt.
    \item \emph{Filter.} From the trailing 104 weekly returns it forecasts
      $\widehat{\mathrm{CVaR}}(w)=c_\alpha\sigma_p(w)-\mu_p(w)$, with
      $c_\alpha=\varphi(\Phi^{-1}(\alpha))/(1-\alpha)$ and $c_{0.95}=2.06$. An over-budget
      proposal is mixed with the trailing minimum-variance portfolio $w^{\mathrm{MV}}_t$,
      keeping as much of the proposal as the budget allows,
      $s^\ast=\max\{s\in[0,1]:\widehat{\mathrm{CVaR}}(s\tilde w_t+(1-s)w^{\mathrm{MV}}_t)\le
      d\}$. It is a safety layer in the sense of [3] and uses data up to week $t-1$ only
      (Fig.~4).
    \item \emph{Projection.} $\Pi_{\mathcal W}$ enforces long-only weights, $w_i\le0.40$ and
      $\lVert w_t-w_{t-1}\rVert_1\le0.50$. The market then pays $r_t$ net of a 5\,bps cost
      per unit of turnover.
    \item \emph{Training.} On random windows of past weeks we back-propagate
      \[
        \mathcal{L}(\theta,\lambda)=-\mathrm{Sharpe}(r)+\lambda\,\mathrm{ReLU}
          \big(\widehat{\mathrm{CVaR}}-d\big)+\kappa\,\mathrm{turnover},\qquad
        \lambda\leftarrow\max\{0,\ \lambda+\eta_\lambda(\widehat{\mathrm{CVaR}}-d)\},
      \]
      where $\widehat{\mathrm{CVaR}}$ is the mean of the worst 5\% of window returns, so its
      gradient reaches only tail weeks. The kept checkpoint has the best validation objective
      among those within the validation CVaR limit.
  \end{enumerate}

  \figcap{Figure 4. The filter on 16 March 2020 for an illustrative equity-heavy proposal.
  (a) Forecast CVaR along the mixing line, with the budget met at $s^\ast=0.34$. (b) Weights
  before and after.}
  \tabcap{Table 1. Training and evaluation details.}
  {\centering\fontsize{19}{23}\selectfont\setlength{\tabcolsep}{3mm}
  \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.12}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}>{\RaggedRight}p{0.24\linewidth}>{\RaggedRight\arraybackslash}p{0.7\linewidth}@{}}
    \toprule
    component & setting \\
    \midrule
    budget & $\alpha=0.95$, $d=1.2\%$ weekly CVaR, up to 5\% of test weeks may breach \\
    dual step & $\eta_\lambda=0.001$ (mis-scaled) or $5.0$ (scaled) \\
    training & 1500 updates per ablation arm and 2500 in the stress study, seeds 7, 13, 23, 42,
      2025 \\
    stress test & train 2008 to 2018, test the 267 weeks from late 2019 to 2024 \\
    walk-forward & train 312, validate 52, test 26 weeks, step 26, 3-seed ensemble per fold \\
    classical baselines & equal weight, inverse volatility, minimum variance, mean-variance,
      risk parity, min-CVaR LP, refit weekly on the trailing 104 weeks (52 for inverse
      volatility) \\
    RL baselines & A2C, PPO (36 configurations) and SAC (8), selected on validation Sharpe \\
    statistics & block bootstrap (2000 draws, block 4) with BCa intervals, Wilcoxon,

  \end{tabular}\par}
}

\block{4\quad Failure 1. The penalty weight never moves}{\body
  \sidefig{0.56}{figure_lambda_trace.pdf}{Figure 5. The penalty weight $\lambda$ during
  training, five seeds.}{With a dual step $\eta_\lambda=10^{-3}$, $\lambda$ stayed near zero
  for all 1500 training updates. The CVaR excess is about $10^{-2}$ per week, so each update
  moved $\lambda$ by about $10^{-5}$. With $\eta_\lambda=5.0$, $\lambda$ rose to between 0.1
  and 0.3. A model-free actor-critic failed in the same way when its cost signal (about
  $10^{-3}$) was left unstandardised next to a unit-scale reward signal.}
  Tail metrics do not reveal the failure. Even with $\lambda$ near zero, the rest of the
  pipeline (the anchor, the projection and checkpoint selection under the CVaR limit) lowered
  $\mathrm{CVaR}_{99}$ by 61\% against the unconstrained learner (0.025 against 0.065,
  permutation test $p=0.0044$).
  \keynote{Plot $\lambda$ during training. A multiplier that stays at zero means the
  constraint is inactive.}
}

\block{5\quad Failure 2. The test decides the ranking}{\body
  \sidefig{0.5}{figure_protocol.pdf}{Figure 6. The same four methods ranked by two tests,
  31-asset universe.}{A walk-forward test retrains the model on a rolling window and evaluates
  it on the next unseen block. On a 31-asset universe, one fixed stress split ranked the
  constrained learner above minimum variance (Sharpe 0.85 against 0.23). A 20-fold
  walk-forward reversed the order (0.74 against 1.40). Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage [5] did not
  change the split result (0.25). The tail-risk reduction held under both tests.}
  \keynote{Compare learners with optimisers on rolling walk-forward folds and report the
  Deflated Sharpe ratio [4].}
}

\block{6\quad The filter keeps the portfolio within budget}{\body
  \sidefig{0.57}{figure_filter_frontier.pdf}{Figure 7. Share of weeks over budget against
  Sharpe on the stress window. The dashed line is the 5\% tolerance.}{A larger dual step made
  $\lambda$ move, but the portfolio was then over budget in 75.1\% of test weeks, although
  classical rules met the same budget in 92\% of weeks. Adding the filter lowered the breach
  rate to \hl{7.4\%}, with $\mathrm{CVaR}_{99}$ of 0.016 and Sharpe 0.851. Raising $\lambda$
  during testing without the filter left it at 81.9\%, so the improvement comes from the
  filter.}
}

\block{7\quad Results across tests}{\body
  We repeated the comparison with and without the constraint in six settings. In a
  walk-forward test the model is retrained every 26 weeks on the previous six years, validated
  on the following year and evaluated on the next 26 unseen weeks. This gives 20 test periods
  (folds) per universe between 2015 and 2024.
  \par\smallskip
  \begin{minipage}[c]{0.49\linewidth}\centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figure_fold_scatter.pdf}
  \end{minipage}\hfill

    Figure 8 plots each of the 40 walk-forward folds as one point.
    \begin{itemize}
      \item The horizontal position is $\mathrm{CVaR}_{0.99}$ without the constraint and the
        vertical position is $\mathrm{CVaR}_{0.99}$ with it.
      \item A point below the diagonal is a period in which the constraint lowered the tail
        loss. 26 of the 27 points off the diagonal lie below it.
      \item Points on the diagonal are periods in which the budget never bound, so both
        models held the same portfolio.
      \item The largest reductions came in the first half of 2020.
    \end{itemize}
  \end{minipage}

\block{8\quad Conclusion}{\body
  The constraint lowered tail risk in a way that survived multiple-testing correction, and
  the filter achieved the out-of-sample compliance that rescaling the dual did not. The
  learner did not beat rolling minimum variance on Sharpe out of sample. The study uses weekly
  data with proportional costs, and the Gaussian forecast can understate fat tails.
