\block{Abstract}{\body
  A portfolio allocator trained by reinforcement learning (RL) to maximise return has no reason
  to avoid large losses. The usual safeguard caps Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR), the average
  loss in the worst weeks, through a Lagrangian penalty. We show that this safeguard can fail
  silently in two ways. When the penalty and the reward sit on different scales, the penalty
  weight stays at zero and the budget is never enforced, even as tail metrics improve. And the
  evaluation protocol can decide the winner, since a single stress window and a rolling
  walk-forward rank the same methods in opposite orders. We add a decision-time filter that
  moves each portfolio toward minimum variance when its forecast CVaR exceeds the budget. It
  cuts the out-of-sample breach rate from \hl{75.1\% to 7.4\%}, and across a 40-fold,
  two-universe walk-forward the tail-risk reduction is significant
  (\hl{26 of 27} untied folds, $p=8.8\times10^{-6}$).
}

\block{Problem Setup}{\body
  Each week the agent splits capital across seven macro ETFs (SPY, TLT, HYG, DBC, GLD, UUP,
  BIL), long-only with max weight 0.40, turnover cap 0.50 and a 5\,bps proportional cost.
  The data are 887 weekly returns from 2008 to 2024. $\mathrm{CVaR}_{0.95}$ is the mean loss
  in the worst 5\% of weeks, and the budget is $d=1.2\%$. The policy is trained by
  back-propagating through the portfolio return itself,
  \[
    \mathcal{L} = -\mathrm{Sharpe} + \lambda\,\mathrm{ReLU}\big(\widehat{\mathrm{CVaR}}-d\big)
      + \kappa\,\mathrm{turnover},\qquad
    \lambda \leftarrow \max\{0,\ \lambda + \eta_\lambda(\widehat{\mathrm{CVaR}}-d)\}.
  \]
  \vspace{2mm}
  \centering
  \begin{tikzpicture}[
      box/.style={draw=dark, line width=1.2mm, rounded corners=3mm, align=center,
        text width=100mm, minimum height=50mm, inner sep=4mm, font=\normalsize, fill=white},
      ours/.style={box, draw=navy, fill=tint, line width=2mm},
      arr/.style={-{Latex[length=9mm, width=9mm]}, line width=1.6mm, color=dark},
      node distance=18mm and 16mm]
    \node[box] (s) {\textbf{State $s_t$}\\ momentum, volatility, weights, drawdown, CVaR};
    \node[box, right=of s] (a) {\textbf{Residual actor}\\
      $w=\mathrm{softmax}(\log a_t + f_\theta(s_t))$, $a_t$ inverse-vol anchor};
    \node[ours, right=of a] (f) {\textbf{\color{navy}CVaR filter (ours)}\\
      if $c_\alpha\sigma_p-\mu_p>d$:\\ $w \leftarrow s\,w + (1-s)\,w^{\mathrm{MV}}$};
    \node[box, below=of f] (p) {\textbf{Projection}\\ weight cap, turnover cap, long-only};
    \node[box, below=of a] (m) {\textbf{Market}\\ $r_t = w_t^{\top}\rho_t - c\lVert\Delta w_t\rVert_1$};
    \draw[arr] (s) -- (a);
    \draw[arr] (a) -- (f);
    \draw[arr] (f) -- (p);
    \draw[arr] (p) -- (m);
    \draw[arr, color=mid, dashed] (m.north) -- node[left, font=\small, text=dark,
      align=right] {exact gradient\\ to $\theta$} (a.south);
  \end{tikzpicture}
  \par\RaggedRight\smallskip
  {\small\color{dark} The filter forecasts CVaR from the trailing 104 weeks ($c_{0.95}=2.06$)
  and keeps the largest $s$ that clears $d$. It uses only data up to $t-1$, and a
  shifted-returns test confirms no look-ahead.\par}
}

\block{Why a Tail Limit}{\body
  In the week of 16 March 2020 the S\&P 500 fell about 15\% and the VIX closed at 82.69. An
  allocator trained only for return kept holding equities. An identical allocator with a CVaR
  limit had already rotated into Treasuries and cash, and lost about half as much
  (\hl{7.7\% vs 16.3\%} drawdown).
  \fig[0.9\linewidth]{figure_stress_paths.pdf}
  \figcap{Figure 1. Drawdown on the 2020--2024 stress window (five-seed mean in bold).}
}

\block{Failure 1: The Penalty Never Switches On}{\body
  The CVaR excess is about $10^{-2}$. With a dual step $\eta_\lambda=10^{-3}$, each update
  moves $\lambda$ by about $10^{-5}$, and 1500 updates are not enough. A model-free
  actor--critic fails the same way when its cost advantage is left at $10^{-3}$ against a
  standardised reward advantage.
  \fig[0.9\linewidth]{figure_lambda_trace.pdf}
  \figcap{Figure 2. The Lagrange multiplier over training (five seeds, mean in bold).}
  The rest of the pipeline already cuts $\mathrm{CVaR}_{99}$ by \hl{61\%} with
  $\lambda\approx0$ ($p=0.0044$), so tail metrics improve whether or not the dual works.
  \diag{\textbf{Diagnostic.} Plot $\lambda$ over training. If it stays at zero, the constraint
  is off.}
}

\block{Failure 2: The Test Protocol Picks the Winner}{\body
  On a 31-asset universe, one stress split ranks the learner above every optimiser. A 20-fold
  rolling walk-forward reverses the ranking. Ledoit--Wolf shrinkage does not explain it. The
  tail reduction against unconstrained RL holds under both protocols
  ($p=6\times10^{-4}$), while the edge over the optimiser does not.
  \fig[0.66\linewidth]{figure_protocol.pdf}
  \figcap{Figure 3. Sharpe ratio of the same methods under two protocols.}
  \diag{\textbf{Diagnostic.} Rank learners against optimisers on rolling walk-forward folds,
  with Deflated Sharpe.}
}

\block{The Fix: A Decision-Time CVaR Filter}{\body
  Scaling the dual fixes training but not the test window. The scaled dual binds in training
  and still breaches in 75\% of test weeks, and four rules for setting $d$ all give 71--78\%.
  The filter acts on the portfolio itself, before it trades.
  \fig[0.68\linewidth]{figure_filter_frontier.pdf}
  \figcap{Figure 4. Breach rate against Sharpe on the stress window. Dashed line, 5\% tolerance.}
  \centering
  \begin{tabular}{@{}lrrr@{}}
    \toprule
    arm (5-seed mean) & Sharpe & $\mathrm{CVaR}_{99}$ & breach \\
    \midrule
    A1 unconstrained & 0.626 & 0.065 & 100.0\% \\
    A2 mis-scaled dual & 0.987 & 0.025 & 7.9\% \\
    A3 scaled dual & 0.911 & 0.028 & 75.1\% \\
    A7 adaptive dual, no filter & 0.826 & 0.031 & 81.9\% \\
    \hl{A6 filter + scaled dual} & \hl{0.851} & \hl{0.016} & \hl{7.4\%} \\
    \midrule
    minimum variance & 0.901 & & 7.9\% \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \par\RaggedRight\smallskip
  A7 raises $\lambda$ online and still breaches, so the gain in A6 comes from the filter.
}

}

\block{Limitations and Takeaways}{\body
  The learner does not beat rolling minimum variance on Sharpe out of sample, and Deflated
  Sharpe removes its Sharpe edge. What survives correction is lower tail risk and budget
  compliance. The study uses weekly data and proportional costs, and the Gaussian forecast can
  understate fat tails.
  \par\medskip
  \iconitem{\textbf{Plot $\lambda$.} A flat trace at zero means the constraint is off.}\par
  \iconitem{\textbf{Match the scales.} Standardise the cost advantage and size $\eta_\lambda$
    to the CVaR excess.}\par
  \iconitem{\textbf{Test on rolling folds,} with Deflated Sharpe, before claiming an edge.}\par
  \iconitem{\textbf{Enforce the budget at trade time.} A soft penalty only discourages breaches
    on average.}\par
